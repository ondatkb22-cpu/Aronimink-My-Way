The 108th PGA Championship

Aronimink Golf Club · Newtown Square, Pennsylvania · May 14–17, 2026
Build Phase 2 Friday, May 8, 2026
DK salaries · 154 players

For the first time since Gary Player’s 1962 victory, the PGA Championship returns to Aronimink Golf Club, the Donald Ross 1928 design that the architect himself called his masterpiece. After decades of well-meaning meddling — Wilson, Fazio, Trent Jones — Gil Hanse and Jim Wagner have restored the course back toward Ross’s original vision.

The Hanse restoration is the central fact of this week. Old PGA-era setups crowded fairways with rough; the modern Aronimink leans on angles, slope, and bunkering instead. The radar plot tells the story plainly: driving accuracy weight is below tour average, but approach play is among the highest weighted on Tour.

Three of Aronimink’s four par-3s play longer than 210 yards. When the Tour last visited (2018 BMW), three of the four hardest holes were par-3s — and the par-3s averaged +0.73 over par when the AT&T National played here in 2011. Long iron play wins this week.

The 18th was extended to 490 yards in the 2025 prep work, joining Merion and Oakmont in the “bruising par-4 closer” category. Holes 11 and 12 may decide the tournament — both are short, but the 11th has the most severely sloped green on a course full of severely sloped greens.

Skill Weighting · Aronimink GC

Aronimink Tour Avg

The shape of victory.

Read the radar carefully. The two longest spokes are distance and approach. Accuracy and around-green are pinched. This is not a “fairway-finder” course in the modern Tour sense.

01
Approach is king. The 0.85 weight is among the highest on Tour. Long-iron proximity matters more than greens-in-regulation rate, because Ross greens punish the wrong tier.
02
Distance over accuracy. Hanse widened the lines compared to old PGA setups. Bombers can attack; precision-only types lose their edge.
03
Par-3 specialists win. Three par-3s over 210 yards. Cameron Young leads Tour in par-3 SG; Adam Scott, Aberg, and Straka are top-10. Watch for these names.
04
Putting is leverage, not skill. Putting weight 0.50 — neutral. Severe slopes mean lag matters; pure stroke quality is less rewarded than course feel.

The Aronimink Thesis

What we lock · What we fade
One-line read: Aronimink is a long, iron-heavy, par-70 Donald Ross course where distance and long-iron play matter most, accuracy matters less than usual, and severe greens mean proximity beats GIR. The play is ball-strikers, especially par-3 specialists, with bonus weight on anyone with a hot recent stretch.

What we lock in

  • Cameron Young — #1 par-3 SG on Tour, +2.12 Total SG, won Cadillac, T7 Truist. The single best skill-to-price ratio in the field.
  • Tommy Fleetwood — +2.10 SG (T-3), 96% cut rate, course profile fits him perfectly.
  • Hideki Matsuyama — Elite irons + 96% cut rate. Aronimink fit elite.
  • Tyrrell Hatton — 100% cut rate, irons intact, major-tested.
  • Matt Fitzpatrick — 100% cut rate, T8 PGA25 last year (Quail Hollow).
  • Ludvig Aberg — +1.63 vs-baseline, top-10 par-3 SG, 4 top-15s in L5 (Lesson 18 LOCKED-IN tag).

Five Intriguing Angles

Where the model and market disagree

Cameron Young at $10,200 is the best price in the field.

$10,200 · +2.12 Total SG · 95% cut rate · +1600 outright

Second-best Total SG in the field (only Rory McIlroy is higher). #1 in PGA Tour par-3 scoring — and Aronimink has four par-3s, three of them over 210 yards. He won Cadillac, shot -3 R1 Truist, and his 95% cut rate provides a real floor.

The math: Scheffler is $13,900 with the same Total SG. Young at $10,200 puts you in 71% of Scheffler’s salary for the same skill. Cap relief lets you build the rest of the roster.

Fleetwood at $9,700 is criminally underpriced.

$9,700 · +2.10 Total SG · 96% cut rate

Same Total SG as Scheffler. Same cut rate as Hatton. Better odds than Aberg and Fitzpatrick despite identical skill. The radar plot is built for him — long, elite irons, neutral putting (which doesn’t hurt because his weakness is the lowest-weighted skill on the course).

Hit -4 R1 Truist last week. Form is right. If you’re playing one ticket, this is the value play.

Jake Knapp at $7,800 is the contrarian dart of the slate — if healthy.

$7,800 · +2.01 Total SG · 7 top-10s · ⚠ Thumb status

+2.01 Total SG places him fifth in the field. At $7,800, the salary treats him like a $9K mid-tier guy. The injury is the only reason this number exists. He withdrew from Cadillac and Truist with a thumb sprain.

Critical Wednesday check: if he tees it up, he is the single best leverage play on the slate.

Keegan Bradley actually won here.

$8,000 · 2018 BMW Championship winner at Aronimink

The only player in this field who has actually won a competition at Aronimink. The 2018 BMW was rain-soaked and ended Monday, so the broader read is muddled — but he knows the greens, knows the angles, and won going away (-20 over 72 holes).

Course history is a real signal at venues with limited Tour exposure.

Adam Scott vs Sepp Straka — same archetype, different prices.

Scott $7,500 vs Straka $8,000 · Both top-10 par-3 SG

Both players are top-10 in PGA Tour par-3 SG, the single most predictive Aronimink-specific stat. Scott had a +5 R1 Truist disaster; Straka had a -5 R1 Truist firework.

Straka is paying you 7% more for the player with current form. Take Straka.
